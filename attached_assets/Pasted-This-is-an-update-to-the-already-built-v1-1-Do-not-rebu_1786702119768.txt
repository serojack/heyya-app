This is an update to the already-built v1.1. Do not rebuild the whole app. Just add and change the following features.
The Big Change: Natural Language for Everyone
In v1.1, we had slash commands like /applyleave. Forget that. In v1.2, anyone in the room — parent, student, or teacher — just types a normal sentence in the chat. No commands. No forms. Just talk like a human.
The built-in Heyya Assistant reads the message, figures out what they want, and replies with a smart card that extracts all the details.
How Leave Works Now (The New Flow)
Example: Mr. Jordanis (Laurel's dad) opens the Grade 10 Class 03 room and types:
"Leave application for Laurel today 8th September 2026, medical reason, seeing the doctor for eye infection, will produce MC, probably will not be able to attend school for 2-3 days. Will update accordingly."
That's it. One long normal sentence.
What the Assistant does:
Parses the message automatically. It pulls out:
Who: Laurel Jordanis
When: 8th September 2026 (and figures out "today" if they say that)
Type: Medical leave
Reason: Seeing the doctor for eye infection
Duration: 2–3 days
Notes: Will produce medical certificate, will update accordingly
Posts a Confirmation Card in the chat that looks like this:
Leave Request Detected
Student: Laurel Jordanis
Date: 8 September 2026
Type: Medical
Reason: Seeing doctor for eye infection
Duration: 2–3 days
Notes: Will produce MC. Will update accordingly.
[Confirm & Submit] [Edit Details] [Cancel]
Mr. Jordanis taps "Confirm & Submit." Done. No typing after that. One tap.
The system immediately:
Records Laurel as on medical leave for those dates
Posts a system message: "Medical leave confirmed for Laurel Jordanis (8 Sep – 10 Sep)"
Notifies the teacher (Ms. Yolanda) with a clean summary
Triggers a "Hook" (see below)
The "Hook" — Automatic Catch-Up Reminder
This is the new killer feature. Whenever a student is confirmed on leave, the Assistant automatically generates a Hook — a little notepad-style reminder message — and drops it privately to both the student AND the parent.
The Hook appears as a special message card and contains:
What they missed today: A summary of what the teacher covered in class (pulled from the room's chat history or any lesson notes posted that day)
Assignments due: Any homework, projects, or tests announced in the room while they were away
Upcoming deadlines: Things due soon that they should keep an eye on
A note: "Feel better, Laurel! Here's what you missed today. Tap any item to mark it done."
Example Hook for Laurel:
Your Hook — Thu, 8 Sep
📌 What we covered today:
Math: Quadratic equations — factoring method
Science: Lab safety review, started Chapter 4
📝 Assignments due:
Math worksheet (due Mon 11 Sep)
Science lab prep questions (due Fri 9 Sep)
✅ Upcoming:
Science quiz — Fri 16 Sep
Get well soon! Tap ✅ to mark items complete.
How the Hook works:
It appears in the chat as a special card (different color from normal messages — maybe a soft yellow or cream background so it stands out)
Both Laurel and Mr. Jordanis see their own copy
Each item has a little checkbox. Tapping it strikes through the text
The Hook stays pinned at the top of their personal view until they dismiss it
If the student is away for multiple days, a new Hook is generated each day
For v1.2, since we don't have real lesson data yet, the Hook can pull from:
Recent messages in the room that contain words like "homework," "assignment," "due," "worksheet," "quiz," "test," "project"
Any message posted by a teacher that looks like a lesson summary
If nothing is found, the Hook says: "No assignments posted yet. Check back later or ask your teacher."
Other Things the Assistant Should Parse Naturally
The Assistant should listen for keywords and patterns in every message, not just leave requests. Here are the main ones:
Table
If someone types...	The Assistant should...
"Leave application for [Name] today..."	Extract all details and post a Confirmation Card
"[Name] is sick today" or "Not feeling well"	Ask: "Shall I create a sick leave request for [Name]?" with a quick [Yes] [No] button
"Doctor appointment" or "Dentist visit"	Detect as medical leave, ask for confirmation
"Family trip" or "Traveling"	Detect as personal leave, ask for dates
"Take attendance" or "Roll call" or "Who's here?"	Open the attendance card (same as v1.1)
"Show timetable" or "My schedule"	Show the student's or teacher's timetable view
"Who is free period 3?" or "Who can cover?"	Check the room's timetable and reply with available teachers
"Assignments due?" or "What homework?"	Show a quick list of recent assignments from chat history
Important: The Assistant should not reply to every single message. Only when it detects a clear action keyword. If someone says "Good morning," the Assistant stays quiet.
What Changes from v1.1
Remove:
The /applyleave slash command. Parents and students won't know commands. Natural language only.
Add:
Keyword parser that scans every incoming message for leave-related patterns
Confirmation Card as a new message type — clean, structured, with Confirm/Edit/Cancel buttons
"Hook" message type — notepad-style card with checkboxes, auto-generated on leave confirmation
Smart detection for medical vs personal vs family leave based on words like "doctor," "sick," "fever," "dentist," "family," "trip," "travel," "wedding," etc.
