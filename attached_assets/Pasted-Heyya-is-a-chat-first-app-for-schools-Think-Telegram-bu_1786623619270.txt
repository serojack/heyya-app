Heyya is a chat-first app for schools. Think Telegram, but instead of messaging your friends, you're managing your entire school day through group chats. Every room is a "Room," every person in it is a "Mate," and every action — attendance, leave, timetables — happens by chatting, not filling out forms.
The magic rule: Nobody ever fills out a form. If a teacher wants to take attendance, they just chat. If a parent wants to report their kid sick, they just chat. If an admin wants to reshuffle a timetable, they just ask the AI assistant in the chat. The app should feel like texting a really smart colleague who happens to handle all your admin work.
Who's Using This?
Four types of people:
Admins — Principals, timetable coordinators, office staff. They can see everything, create rooms, approve things, and manage the big picture.
Teachers — They take attendance, apply for leave, check their timetables, and cover classes for absent colleagues.
Students — They can see their own timetable, check their attendance record, and view announcements in their class rooms.
Parents — They can see their child's attendance, apply for leave on their behalf, and get updates from class rooms.
Everyone logs in, gets assigned a role, and only sees what they're allowed to see.
The Layout: Make It Look Like Telegram
I want the exact vibe of Telegram. Clean, fast, familiar.
Left sidebar: A list of Rooms. Each room has a little avatar picture, the room name underneath, and a preview of the last message sent there. Just like your chat list in Telegram. At the top of this sidebar, put a search bar to find rooms quickly.
Center: The chat itself. Big open space. Messages bubble in from left and right. System messages (like "Attendance is now open") sit in the middle with a soft grey background.
Bottom: A message input bar. It grows if you type a lot. Has a paperclip icon for uploading files, and a send button.
Right side (on desktop only): A "Mates" panel showing who's in this room right now, their little green dot if they're online, and their role. On mobile, this becomes a slide-out menu.
Colors: Use Telegram blue (#0088cc) as your main accent. Clean whites and light greys for light mode. A nice dark navy (#17212b) for dark mode. Everything should feel airy and uncluttered.
Fonts: Keep it simple. Inter or the system font. Nothing fancy.
Rooms: The Heart of Everything
Rooms are group chats. But they're organized by purpose:
Class Rooms — "Year 7 Science," "Grade 5A," etc. Teachers and students are in here. Parents can view but usually don't chat.
Staff Rooms — For teachers and admin only. School-wide announcements, staff leave, general chatter.
Admin Rooms — Private spaces for principals and coordinators to manage timetables and see AI recommendations.
When you tap a room, you enter the chat. That's where all the work happens.
Creating a room: An admin taps the "New Room" button, gives it a name, picks what type it is, and adds people. Done.
Feature 1: Taking Attendance (The Chat Way)
Here's how a teacher takes attendance on a Monday morning:
They open their class room, say: "Take attendance" or type /takeattendance.
Immediately, the app posts a special message in the chat — an Attendance Card. It looks like a message bubble, but inside it's a grid of all the students' little round profile pictures with their names underneath. Next to each face are four small buttons: Present (green), Absent (red), Late (yellow), Excused (blue).
Everyone starts as Present by default. The teacher just taps the exceptions. "Jamie is absent, tap." "Sarah is late, tap."
As they tap, the colors update live. Other teachers in the room can see it happening in real-time if they're watching.
When done, the teacher taps a "Done" button at the bottom of the card. The card updates to show: "Attendance finalized. 28 Present, 2 Absent, 1 Late." It becomes a record in the chat history. Anyone can scroll back and see it.
If a parent opens the room later, they can see their own child's status, but they can't see the other kids' names or statuses. Privacy matters.
The rule: Attendance is never a spreadsheet you open. It's a conversation you have in the room.
Feature 2: Applying for Leave (Just Text It)
Teachers, students, or parents should be able to message the room like they're texting a friend.
Example:
A teacher types: "Hey, I'm feeling really rough today. Got a fever. Won't make it in."
The app is smart. It detects this is a sick leave request. It replies immediately with a Leave Card — a neat little box in the chat that says:
Leave Request — Bob
Type: Sick Leave
Date: Today (15 March 2026)
Reason: "Feeling really rough, got a fever."
Status: Pending Approval
Underneath are two buttons: Approve and Reject. Admin sees this in the Staff Room or Admin Room and taps one.
If approved, two things happen:
Bob gets a notification: "Your sick leave for today has been approved."
The AI assistant wakes up and checks: "Bob has 3 classes scheduled today. Let me find someone to cover."
Parents do the same thing. A parent jumps into the class room and says: "Emma has a dentist appointment tomorrow morning, she'll be in after lunch." The app creates a leave card for Emma. Teacher sees it, taps approve. Done.
If someone wants to be specific, they can say: "I need personal leave from March 20th to March 22nd for a family event." The app understands the dates, the type, and the reason. No dropdown menus. No date pickers. Just talk.
Feature 3: The AI Assistant — Your Smart Colleague
Every room has access to an AI assistant. It looks like another user in the chat, but its avatar is a little robot icon and its name is "Heyya" or "Assistant." It should feel friendly and helpful, not robotic. Like that organized colleague who always knows the schedule.
What it does:
When a teacher calls in sick and it's approved, the Assistant automatically posts a message:
"Bob is away today. I've checked the timetable. Here are the best options to cover his classes:"
Then it shows an AI Recommendation Card:
Cover Recommendation
Carol — Free during periods 2 & 4. Teaches Math (matches Bob's Science — cross-coverage possible). Last covered this class: 2 weeks ago. [Assign]
David — Free period 2 only. Teaches Science. Currently at 4/5 max load. [Assign]
External Supply — No ideal internal match for period 3. [Book External]
Admin taps Assign next to Carol. Carol gets a notification: "You've been assigned to cover Year 8 Science, Period 2. Accept?" She taps yes. The timetable updates. Everyone in the room sees the change as a system message.
The Assistant also answers questions:
A teacher can ask: "Who's free period 3 on Tuesdays?"
The Assistant replies: "Carol, David, and Emma have no classes scheduled period 3 Tuesdays."
Or: "When's the best time for a staff meeting this week?"
Assistant: "Wednesday period 5 looks clear for 85% of staff. Want me to block it?"
Feature 4: Timetables & Rosters (Visual and Drag-Friendly)
Schools need timetables. But again, no boring grid software. Here's how it works:
In any room, someone can say: "Show the timetable" or tap a timetable icon. The chat area transforms into a week view — Monday to Friday across the top, periods down the side. Each slot is a colored block showing the subject, teacher, and room number.
For the timetable coordinator:
They can drag blocks around. If they drag "Year 8 Math" from Monday Period 1 to Tuesday Period 2, the app checks: "Is the room free? Is the teacher free? Does it clash?" If there's a problem, the block gets a red border and a little warning: "Mrs. Johnson is already in Room 201 at this time."
AI-Assisted Timetable Building:
The coordinator can upload an Excel file with constraints — teacher names, subjects they can teach, maximum periods per week, rooms, class sizes. Then they type in the chat: "Heyya, can you build me a draft timetable where no teacher has back-to-back classes?"
The Assistant thinks, then posts a Timetable Card — a preview of the generated week. The coordinator can drag to tweak it, and when happy, taps "Publish." The timetable goes live, and every affected room gets a system message: "New timetable published. Check your updated schedule."
Personal view: Every user has a "My Timetable" view they can access from their profile or by asking the Assistant. It shows their schedule across all rooms.
Feature 5: Bulk Upload (Excel & CSV)
Sometimes schools have existing data — a big list of students, or last year's timetable. We need to handle that.
The flow:
An admin drags an Excel file into the chat, or taps the paperclip and uploads it. The file appears as a message: "📎 students_import.xlsx (45KB)" with a "Processing..." spinner.
The app reads the file. Then it posts a preview message in the chat — a little table showing the first 3 rows it found:
Preview: students_import.xlsx
Table
Name	Email	Grade	Parent Email
John Doe	john@school.edu	7A	parent@email.com
Sarah Smith	sarah@school.edu	7A	mom@email.com
Found 45 students. Import into "Year 7A" room?
[Import All] [Check for Issues] [Cancel]
Admin taps Import All. Done. The app creates all the user accounts, adds them to the room, and posts: "✅ 45 students imported successfully."
If there are problems — like duplicate emails or missing names — the app says: "Found 3 issues. Want to review?" and shows which rows need fixing.
Supported files:
Student lists (CSV or Excel)
Teacher lists with subjects
Full timetable exports
Attendance records from other systems
Feature 6: Documents & Files
Any file shared in a room stays in that room. Photos, PDFs, Word docs, Excel sheets. They appear as chat messages with a file icon, name, size, and a download button.
There's a Documents tab in each room (maybe as a toggle next to the chat, or accessible via a folder icon). It shows every file ever shared in that room, searchable by name. Think of it like the "Shared Media" section in Telegram.
Admins can pin important documents so they stay at the top.
How the Chat Should Feel (The Vibe)
This is the most important part. The entire app is a chat app that happens to do ERP stuff.
Messages are everything. When attendance is taken, it's a message. When leave is approved, it's a message. When the timetable changes, it's a system message. The chat history is the audit trail.
Interactive cards live in messages. Those attendance grids, leave request boxes, and timetable previews? They're special message types. They look like bubbles, but they're clickable and functional.
Slash commands for power users. Type / and see options: /takeattendance, /applyleave, /timetable, /ai. But regular natural language should work too.
Typing indicators. When the AI is thinking, show the little bouncing dots.
Read receipts. In staff rooms, show who's seen important announcements.
Reactions. Let people react to messages with emojis. A principal might react 👍 to a leave request instead of typing "Approved."
The Rules (Write These Down)
No forms. Ever. If a user needs to provide 5 pieces of information, the app asks 5 questions in the chat, one by one, or shows an interactive card. No pop-up modals with input fields. No "New Entry" pages. Chat only.
Everything happens in a room. There is no global dashboard you go to. You open a room, and you work there. The room is your workspace.
Mobile first, desktop friendly. On a phone, the sidebar collapses to a bottom navigation bar: Rooms | Timetable | AI | Profile. On desktop, you see the full three-panel layout.
Privacy by default. Students can't see other students' attendance. Parents only see their own kids. Teachers see their classes and staff rooms.
Smart defaults. Attendance starts as "present" — teachers only mark exceptions. Leave starts as "today" if no date is given. The app should assume the obvious so users type less.
Never silent. Every important action posts a message. If the AI assigns a cover teacher, everyone sees it. If a timetable changes, the room knows. Transparency builds trust.
The Look & Feel Checklist
[ ] Sidebar room list with avatars, names, and last message preview
[ ] Chat bubbles: white for others, light blue for me, grey for system
[ ] Rounded corners on everything. Soft, friendly, not sharp corporate edges
[ ] Dark mode that actually looks good (deep navy, not harsh black)
[ ] Profile pictures everywhere. People, not ID numbers.
[ ] Status dots: green for online, grey for offline, yellow for "on leave"
[ ] Smooth animations. Messages slide in. Cards expand gently. Nothing jarring.
[ ] Empty states that are helpful. "No messages yet. Say hi!" or "No attendance taken today. Type 'take attendance' to start."
Start Building in This Order
Phase 1: Get the bones right. Login, roles, create rooms, join rooms, basic chat messages. Make it feel like Telegram first.
Phase 2: Add the attendance card. Teacher types "take attendance" → card appears → tap to mark → finalize. Store it.
Phase 3: Leave requests. Natural language in → leave card out → approve/reject buttons → notify user.
Phase 4: Bring in the AI. Connect to OpenAI or Claude. Parse messages. Generate replacement recommendations. Post them as cards.
Phase 5: Timetable view. Week grid. Drag and drop. Color blocks. AI generation from uploaded constraints.
Phase 6: File uploads and bulk import. Drag Excel into chat → preview → import.
